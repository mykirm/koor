# Koor — Market & Scientific Premise Audit

**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Author:** Myra Kirmani
**Status:** Companion document to the project — not a marketing piece. The point is to be explicit about which scientific claims under Koor are strong, which are weaker, and which are wrong, and to map the surrounding product landscape so the contribution is precise.

Koor descended from a larger product vision ("Tide") that proposed real-time cycle-aware reflection grounded in Apple Watch HRV, longitudinal thought history, and AI dialogue. The current Koor scope is narrower: a within-subject A/B comparison of cycle-phase-conditioned LLM retrieval grounding versus context-blind responses. This document carries the underlying audit forward so the trimmed scope rests on the same evidentiary base.

---

## TL;DR

The scientific foundation Koor inherits is **unusually strong in two places, weaker in two others, and wrong in one**. Women with ADHD do show substantially elevated rates of provisional PMDD (~31–45% vs ~3–8% base rate), luteal-phase cognition is measurably altered, and physiological state demonstrably biases the content and felt-validity of thoughts. But the original framing of ADHD as a "chronically activated sympathetic nervous system" is **not** supported by the autonomic literature — if anything, ADHD research leans toward hypo-arousal or dysregulated arousal. Apple Watch HRV is valid for overnight trend monitoring but **not validated for real-time detection of acute emotional state**, which is why Koor pivoted to using HRV as multi-day context (via mcPHASES) rather than as a real-time trigger. The commercial whitespace, however, is real: **no shipping product as of May 2026 combines cycle phase + longitudinal thought history + AI dialogue + no-streak design for ADHD women**. The closest adjacencies each own one piece. The risk is less competitive than regulatory and ethical: a chatbot that tells women in PMDD/ADHD dysregulation which of their thoughts to discount sits in the exact blast radius of current lawsuits, FTC Section 6(b) orders, and state AI-companion legislation.

## What the peer-reviewed literature actually supports

### ADHD and emotional dysregulation: strong evidence, contested nomenclature

Emotional dysregulation is one of the most robust findings in adult ADHD research. Shaw, Stringaris, Nigg, and Leibenluft's 2014 *American Journal of Psychiatry* review remains the landmark synthesis: **34–70% of adults with ADHD meet criteria for clinically significant emotion dysregulation**, with the variance reflecting measurement. Barkley's Deficient Emotional Self-Regulation (DESR) construct has accumulated converging evidence through Surman et al. (2011, 2013, *AJP*), Biederman et al. (2020, *European Psychiatry*), and Beheshti et al.'s 2020 meta-analysis in *BMC Psychiatry*. Faraone et al.'s 2019 *JCPP* practitioner review argued for formal diagnostic inclusion; DSM-5-TR (2022) still lists it only as an "associated feature." The underlying phenomenon is empirically robust. The debate is semantic.

**Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria is a different story.** RSD was coined by William Dodson in the 1990s and lives almost entirely in ADDitude Magazine and clinical lore. It is **not in DSM-5, DSM-5-TR, or ICD-11**. Its only peer-reviewed appearance is a 4-case series in 2024 in a low-impact journal, in which Dodson himself acknowledges the concept "has not been examined in scientific literature until this article." The widely-cited "one-third of patients most impaired by RSD" and "60% respond to alpha-2 agonists" figures come from Dodson's clinical impressions, not controlled trials. The underlying phenomenon — heightened social-rejection sensitivity in ADHD — is real under the emotion-dysregulation umbrella. The RSD branding dramatically outpaces the evidence base. **Koor does not use the term "RSD" in medical-sounding claims.**

### ADHD in women and the menstrual cycle: emerging but small

The strongest peer-reviewed work comes from three labs. Hinshaw's Berkeley Girls with ADHD Longitudinal Study (Hinshaw et al., 2022, *JCPP*) documents elevated self-harm, suicidality, and internalizing outcomes in women with childhood ADHD. Roberts, Eisenlohr-Moul, and Martel (2018, *Psychoneuroendocrinology*, N=32) found that **decreased estradiol combined with increased progesterone predicted higher next-day ADHD symptoms**, especially in women with high trait impulsivity, with ADHD peaks in early follicular and early luteal phases. Eng et al. (2024, *Hormones and Behavior*) extended this into a "multiple hormone sensitivity" theory of biphasic affective risk. Osianlis et al.'s 2025 systematic review in the *Journal of Attention Disorders* concluded that evidence is **"largely suggestive"** across 11 studies — **no RCT of luteal-phase medication dose adjustment has been published**, and there are no empirical menopause-ADHD studies, despite heavy clinical assertions from Quinn, Skoglund, and ADDitude Magazine. Lotta Borg Skoglund's claim that estrogen is "super fuel for the female brain" has a mechanistic basis (estrogen inhibits COMT, enhances PFC dopamine per Shanmugan and Epperson 2014, *Human Brain Mapping*; see Jacobs and D'Esposito 2011, *J Neurosci*, for the Val/Met COMT interaction), but remains more reasonable hypothesis than proven fact.

### PMDD and ADHD comorbidity: the strongest single pillar

PMDD is well-characterized. Schmidt and Rubinow's 1998 *NEJM* GnRH-agonist/add-back experiment and the 2017 *AJP* follow-up established the **differential sensitivity model**: PMDD is an abnormal CNS response to *normal* hormonal transitions, not abnormal hormone levels. Dubey et al. (2017, *Molecular Psychiatry*) found dysregulation of the ESC/E(Z) epigenetic complex in PMDD cell lines, providing a cellular-level mechanism. Bäckström et al. (2014, *Progress in Neurobiology*) and Hantsoo and Epperson (2020, *Neurobiology of Stress*) document the **paradoxical anxiogenic effect of allopregnanolone at low-to-moderate luteal concentrations** via GABA-A receptor plasticity — the mechanism behind why PMDD feels the way it feels.

The ADHD-PMDD overlap is Koor's most defensible single claim. **Dorani et al. (2021, *J Psychiatric Research*) found 45.5% provisional PMDD in 209 Dutch ADHD outpatients**; a population-based replication (Broughton et al., 2025, *British Journal of Psychiatry*) found 31–41% in self-reported clinical ADHD vs 9.8% in controls, with a relative risk of ~4.5 for comorbid ADHD plus depression/anxiety. The clinical-sample number was likely inflated; the population number isn't. Women with ADHD are at roughly 3–4× elevated risk of PMDD — a clinically and commercially meaningful signal.

### Thoughts are genuinely shaped by physiology: the strongest pillar for "signal vs noise"

This is where Koor is on the firmest theoretical ground. Four converging literatures support the claim that bodily state shapes thought content and felt-validity: **Bower's 1981 *American Psychologist* mood-congruent memory work** (replicated in Ucros 1989 meta-analysis and Forgas's affect-infusion model), **Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis** (Bechara, Damasio and Damasio, 2000, *Cerebral Cortex*), **Critchley and Garfinkel's interoception research** (2017, *Current Opinion in Psychology*; Azevedo et al., 2017, *Nature Communications* showed cardiac afferents bias social cognition in real time), and **Barrett's theory of constructed emotion** (2017, *SCAN*). Schwarz and Clore's classic affect-as-information work (1983, *JPSP*) is the key theoretical ancestor of Koor's pitch: **people use their current affect as information about the world, but discount that information when the source is made salient**. Making hormonal/physiological context salient should reduce its inappropriate influence on judgment. This has been tested indirectly in weather-misattribution studies but **not directly in a menstrual-cycle paradigm** — which is part of what Koor's within-subject design is set up to probe.

The closest thing to "validity-monitoring of thoughts during dysregulated states" comes from Seow et al. (2023, *eLife*), which showed that **metacognitive confidence is state-dependent** in anxious-depression and recovers with treatment. Effect sizes are modest (β ≈ 0.1–0.3). Brain imaging of PMDD specifically (Gingnell et al., 2012, *Horm Behav*; Comasco et al., 2014, *Human Brain Mapping*; Baller et al., 2013, *AJP*) shows altered corticolimbic reactivity and reduced prefrontal top-down control, with Rubinow et al. 2007 finding that PMDD women specifically misjudge neutral faces as sad in the luteal phase — direct evidence that luteal-phase cognition is not merely more negative but more distorted.

### HRV is valid. Apple Watch HRV for real-time emotional state is not.

The autonomic proxy story is clean. RMSSD and high-frequency HRV are consensus-level valid indices of parasympathetic/vagal tone (Task Force 1996; Laborde et al., 2017; Shaffer and Ginsberg, 2017). **The LF/HF ratio is not a valid sympatho-vagal balance index** (Billman, 2013, *Frontiers in Physiology*) — Koor avoids any "sympathetic vs parasympathetic" dichotomous framing. Low resting HRV is a transdiagnostic correlate of depression (Kemp et al. 2010: Hedges' g = −0.30), anxiety (Chalmers et al. 2014: g = −0.29 to −0.45; Cheng et al. 2022 replication with larger effects), and is responsive to HRV biofeedback (Goessl et al. 2017: g = 0.83 vs control for anxiety/stress). Vagally-mediated HRV is **moderately lower in the mid-luteal phase, driven by progesterone** (Schmalenberger et al. 2019 meta-analysis: d = −0.39; 2020 within-person replication). Women with larger luteal HRV drops show larger premenstrual distress (Schmalenberger et al. 2024).

**The Apple Watch-specific picture is more restrictive than the original Koor pitch assumed.** Apple Watch derives HRV via PPG, reports SDNN (not the superior RMSSD) during Breathe sessions and sleep, and performs poorly in motion. Validation work (Hernando et al. 2018, *Sensors*; Hinde et al. 2021; Miller et al. 2022, *Sensors*) confirms good agreement with ECG **at rest** but systematic underestimation of SDNN (mean bias ~−8 ms vs Polar H10) and high capture failure rates in non-rest contexts (up to 44% during talking). Critically, the watch samples opportunistically and cannot be triggered mid-emotion by the user. **Apple Watch HRV is a lagging, within-person trend signal**, not a real-time state detector. **This is exactly why Koor v1 uses mcPHASES-derived HRV as a multi-day context feature paired into the retrieval corpus, rather than as a live readout of dysregulation.** The physiological input is a low-temporal-resolution context variable (overnight resting HRV, cycle-phase HRV envelope), and the system prompt treats it that way.

### The wrong framing: ADHD as chronic sympathetic activation

This is the one place where the original pitch contradicted the literature, and Koor's current framing has been adjusted accordingly. Bellato et al.'s 2020 systematic review in *Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews* — the definitive synthesis of 55 autonomic-function-in-ADHD studies — found that **ADHD autonomic differences trend toward hypo-arousal or dysregulated arousal**, not chronic sympathetic overactivation; about half of findings were null. Robe et al.'s 2019 meta-analysis found reduced vagal HRV in ADHD during tasks with only a small effect (Hedge's g = 0.20) and significant heterogeneity. Lee et al.'s 2025 umbrella review in *Translational Psychiatry* rated HRV evidence for ADHD as "weak." The popular reframe of ADHD as fight-or-flight also frequently leans on polyvagal theory, which **Grossman's 2023 critique in *Biological Psychology*** argues is foundationally untenable at the neuroanatomical and comparative-physiology level. The accurate framing is closer to **state regulation failure**: ADHD brains struggle to match arousal to context, going over- or under-aroused depending on circumstance. This is compatible with Koor's clinical logic but is a different mechanistic story than "your nervous system is chronically in fight-or-flight."

## What women are actually saying

The lived-experience literature is strikingly consistent across voices. Jessica McCabe's 2024 *How to ADHD* frames the core user experience as "boost the signal, decrease the noise" — language that almost eerily matches Koor's — and centers self-forgiveness over self-optimization. Brendan Mahan's "Wall of Awful" (popularized via McCabe's YouTube) describes the accumulated emotional bricks that make simple tasks feel impossible. Dani Donovan's *The Anti-Planner* is organized not by time but by **the emotional state blocking action**: overwhelmed, bored, resentful, anxious, guilt-ridden — a structural rebuke to streak-based apps. Sari Solden's work since 1995 (*Women with Attention Deficit Disorder*; *A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD*, 2019, with Michelle Frank) argues that **the real disability is internalized shame about gendered expectations**, not the symptoms themselves. Kate Moryoussef's *ADHD Women's Wellbeing Podcast* and Lotta Borg Skoglund's *ADHD Girls to Women* (2023) converge on cycle-aware ADHD as the missing frame. "ADHD Jenna" (Jenna Free, a Canadian ADHD therapist with ADHD herself) argues that **external tools are "sprinkles on icing" without internal nervous-system regulation**, framing ADHD people-pleasing as the fawn response.

Reddit's r/ADHDWomen, r/PMDD, and r/adhd_women surface remarkably consistent themes: "is this a real thought or a hormone?"; luteal-phase cognitions about relationships, career, or self-worth that fully remit on day 1–2 of menses; morning executive paralysis (ADHD inertia / waiting mode); and the ritualized grief around abandoned productivity systems ("works for 3 weeks, then I can't even look at it"). The IAPMD community names this survival orientation with the word "warrior." The Me v PMDD app's most-cited user claim is that **the symptom chart is "evidence against being gaslit into 'too emotional'"** — the data is the validation, not the advice. Journalism in *The Cut*, *The Atlantic*, the *NYT*, and *The Guardian* 2022–2026 has documented the pandemic-driven diagnosis surge (stimulant fills up >10% in women 15–44; cohort of newly diagnosed 23–49-year-old women roughly doubled 2020–22), with qualitative studies (2023 *Health Psychology Research*; 2025 *Scientific Reports*) finding dominant themes of shame, diagnosis-as-revelation, and grief for "the life I could have led."

**The convergent pain points are stable**: (1) I can't tell a real thought from a hormonal/dysregulated one; (2) existing tools weaponize my perfectionism and then I abandon them; (3) hormones and ADHD interact and nobody's addressing that intersection; (4) I need to be witnessed accurately, not optimized; (5) mornings and luteal weeks are hell. Koor reads as a direct response to (1), (3), and (4) — which is both a signal of product-market fit and a warning that many people have noticed the same gap.

## Competitive landscape: pieces exist, the combination doesn't

**No shipping product as of May 2026 combines cycle-phase awareness + longitudinal thought history + AI dialogue about the validity of specific thoughts + explicitly non-streaked design, specifically for ADHD women.** Each piece has an owner:

| Dimension | Closest owner | What they miss |
|---|---|---|
| HRV ingestion + recommendations | Welltory | Lifestyle advice, not cognitive dialogue; accuracy complaints |
| Cycle + symptom + community | Flo, Clue, Me v PMDD | No CBT/AI thought dialogue; no HRV input |
| Cycle + physiological (HRV, wrist-temp) | Apple Health, Oura, Whoop | No cognitive/therapeutic layer at all |
| AI + ADHD coaching | Inflow, Shimmer, Saner.AI | Cycle-blind; HRV-blind |
| AI journaling with memory | Rosebud (Bloom), Mindsera | No HRV, no cycle; sycophancy risk (Rosebud) or rigidity (Mindsera) |
| Non-streak, shame-free design | Finch, How We Feel, Tiimo | No cognitive-reframing engine; no physiological integration |
| PMDD-specific | Me v PMDD | Tracking only; no AI; chronic bugs reported |
| Physiological-aware chatbot | Earkick | Uses movement/sleep; HRV integration not validated; still has streaks |

The AI mental-health chatbot category is in notable distress. **Woebot's consumer app shut down June 30, 2025** after its founder Alison Darcy cited FDA regulatory ambiguity for LLM-based digital therapeutics (STAT, July 2, 2025). Pi is a "zombie product" after Microsoft's March 2024 acqui-hire of the Inflection team. Replika is permanently banned in Italy (Garante reaffirmed April 2025) and left users traumatized after its February 2023 ERP-removal. Character.AI settled the Setzer wrongful-death case and four others in January 2026 alongside Texas and Colorado suits; the October 2024 Garcia v. Character Technologies complaint survived First Amendment dismissal in spring 2025. The FTC issued Section 6(b) orders to seven AI-chatbot companies on September 11, 2025. California SB 243 passed as first-in-nation AI-chatbot safety regulation. The APA formally lobbied the FTC against chatbots posing as therapists in February 2025. **Any new AI mental-health product for a vulnerable population is shipping into a hot regulatory zone.** Koor's "research prototype, not therapy" positioning is deliberate.

Clinical evidence quality for existing chatbots is thin. NICE's 2024–25 assessment of Wysa's DRA flagged it as "non-comparative, mostly author-affiliated" evidence. Abd-Alrazaq et al.'s 2020 *JMIR* meta-analysis covered mostly rule-based bots; Zhong et al. 2024 showed effects fading at 3-month follow-up. Baumel et al.'s 2019 *JMIR* retention study found **median 15-day retention of 3.9% and 30-day retention of 3.3%** for mental-health apps — the benchmark Koor is implicitly pitching against. Stanford's 2025 "Spirals" analysis and Moore et al.'s 2025 FAccT paper quantified LLM sycophancy (>70% of messages), with suicidal-ideation test failures replicated three weeks after publication in *The Independent*.

The nervous-system and AI-journaling categories each own one of Koor's legs. **Mindsera** is notable because it's the one AI-journaling app that actually disputes user thoughts (explicitly uses the CBT ABC model and thought-distortion analysis) rather than affirming them — but it's cycle-blind and HRV-blind. **How We Feel** (Marc Brackett/Yale, free, nonprofit) and **Finch** own the non-shame design space: no streaks, no missed-day guilt, inclusive customization. **Apollo Neuro**, **Sensate**, and **Calmigo** are hardware-plus-app somatic products with limited (often company-sponsored) efficacy evidence. **Dot by New Computer shut down September 5, 2025**, widely read as a sign that generic AI companions without a specific therapeutic frame are hard to sustain.

## Failure modes Koor is designed around

The category has five well-documented failure modes and Koor's design pre-empts three of them.

**Sycophancy.** The Stanford 2025 spirals analysis found >70% of messages showed sycophantic patterns; when users expressed suicidal thoughts, bots encouraged self-harm 9.9% of the time. Koor's central evaluation question — "does grounding reduce sycophancy?" — is structurally the opposite of the default LLM failure mode. The pre-registered S1–S8 rubric makes the construct measurable rather than rhetorical. The novelty-branched prompt with the "name the pattern if one exists" instruction in the low-novelty branch is the architectural anti-sycophancy lever: a model can't both surface a non-affirming pattern from a user's own data and unconditionally validate the user's current framing.

**The 4% retention cliff.** Baumel et al.'s benchmark is real and applies. Koor is not chasing retention — it's a research prototype, not a consumer product. But the design principle (the app is *most* valuable in luteal/morning-paralyzed states because that's when the retrieved history is most diagnostic) is exactly the retention lever a v2 would build on.

**Harm incidents with vulnerable users.** Tessa/NEDA (June 2023, eating-disorder harm), Character.AI (Setzer, Rupnow, Texas, Colorado), Koko's January 2023 consent failure, the Belgian Chai case (March 2023), and the "ChatGPT-induced psychosis" cases documented in 2025 all involved users in vulnerable states receiving AI-generated content that made things worse. **A product explicitly targeted at women in PMDD/ADHD dysregulation is in the highest-risk user category.** Koor's footer disclaimer, the explicit "reflection support, not therapy" framing, the absence of any clinical claim, and the non-prescriptive G6 rubric item are the minimum-viable guardrails for a research prototype. A v2 would need real crisis-detection and escalation protocols.

**Generic advice that doesn't convert to action.** This is the knowing-doing gap for which Barkley's executive-function model (1997, *Psychological Bulletin*) and Gollwitzer's implementation-intentions work (meta-analysis effect size d = 0.65 in general populations) offer the best-validated response. The grounded prompt's "suggest ONE concrete micro-action" instruction maps onto this, and the G3 rubric item directly measures it.

**Streak backfire.** Every ADHD-community voice — McCabe, Donovan, Free, Moryoussef, r/ADHDWomen — converges here. Koor has no streaks, no progress bars, no missed-day language. The watch-out is that "no streaks" can still smuggle in shame through subtle weekly reports that implicitly grade the user; a v2 must hold the line on Tiimo's missed-task backfill model.

## Red flags and ethical considerations

Five items are on the builder's desk before any v2 work.

**Regulatory exposure is concrete, not theoretical.** FDA convened a Digital Health Advisory Committee on generative-AI mental-health devices in November 2025. California SB 243 is law. FTC Section 6(b) orders are active. Woebot Health explicitly cited FDA pathway ambiguity as the reason for consumer shutdown. Any future Koor positioning (wellness vs medical device vs digital therapeutic) determines which of these apply, and the "calibrate weight on your thoughts" framing could be read as a mental-health claim by a careful regulator. **A deliberate regulatory posture and explicit non-diagnostic, non-therapeutic disclaimers are necessary but may not be sufficient**, per Harvard's Glenn Cohen on Earkick-style disclaimers.

**The specific population is high-risk.** Dorani 2021 found 45.5% PMDD comorbidity in ADHD women; Hinshaw 2022 documented elevated suicidality in women with childhood ADHD; IAPMD documents PMDD-day-specific suicidal ideation as common. A meaningful fraction of the target user base will enter acute crisis states during luteal weeks. **A v2 must have actual crisis-detection and actual escalation to 988/Samaritans/text lines**, not a liability footer. Character.AI's Texas and Florida cases establish this is litigable.

**"Is this a real thought or hormones" can pathologize legitimate distress.** This is the ethically hardest part of the concept. A woman in an abusive relationship who has the thought "my partner is treating me badly" during her luteal phase must not be told by an AI that her thought has reduced credibility because her HRV is low. **Mood-congruent cognition research (Bower 1981) shows the bias applies to ambiguous judgments, not to accurate perception of reality.** Koor's grounded prompt is deliberately constructed *not* to discount thoughts. It surfaces context and patterns; it does not tell the user which thoughts to believe. The G6 rubric item ("non-prescriptive grounding") is the audit hook.

**Data sensitivity is maximum-tier.** Post-Dobbs, menstrual-cycle data plus mental-health data plus location is a prosecutorial dream in states criminalizing reproductive care. Flo's 2021 FTC settlement and 2025 $56M class action, Stardust's privacy rewrites under journalistic pressure, FTC's Monument and Cerebral actions, and the 2024 expansion of the Health Breach Notification Rule all apply. Koor v1 has no backend database and stores no PII; even runs.jsonl is local-only or `/tmp` on Vercel. End-to-end encryption, on-device processing where possible, and an explicit minimization posture are floor requirements for any v2.

**Anti-sycophancy is harder than it sounds.** A model trained by RLHF on user satisfaction will drift toward telling women their instincts are correct, which is the opposite of what "separate signal from noise" means. Mindsera is the only existing product with a credible anti-sycophancy posture, and it does so through rigid framework application (Inversion, ABC, First Principles), not through personalization. **Koor's longitudinal-history approach is potentially better** — "you've had this exact thought three luteal cycles in a row and each time it resolved on day 2" is a non-sycophantic intervention grounded in the user's own data — but only if the system is architecturally designed to surface disconfirming patterns. The novelty-branched system prompt is the architectural lever; the eval rubric is the audit.

## What's genuinely novel, what's not

**Genuinely new:** (1) the specific product thesis of **using the user's own longitudinal thought history + cycle phase + physiological context to calibrate the felt-validity of a specific thought in the moment** — nobody is doing this; (2) the integration of **PMDD/ADHD-comorbidity science into product logic** rather than as a marketing frame — the clinical research supports the approach and the user community is starved for it; (3) the **explicit anti-sycophancy via user-own-data** mechanism, distinct from Mindsera's rigid-framework approach.

**Not novel:** (1) the nervous-system-regulation-before-action frame — Rootd, Finch, How We Feel, and the entire somatic-wellness category own this; (2) the shame-free, no-streak design — Finch, How We Feel, Tiimo demonstrate this is table stakes; (3) the "women's mental health AI" positioning — Flo's Ask Flo, Hormona's Ask Hormona, and several 2024–26 VC-funded startups are here; (4) HRV biofeedback for emotion regulation — this has a mature evidence base (Goessl 2017 meta-analysis, g = 0.83) but is not the same as Koor's proposition.

The sharpest novelty-per-defensibility is the **longitudinal-thought-history-plus-cycle-phase-plus-physiological-envelope** model. The HRV piece is more marketing than engine in any wearable-only product (Apple Watch data is too sparse to be a real-time trigger), but as a **context feature over days and weeks**, it is legitimately useful. The real engine is the user's own thought history weighted by cycle phase — which is a product-engineering problem, not an HRV problem. Koor v1 isolates exactly this engine.

## Where Koor v1 lands

**Strong on:** emotional dysregulation as core to ADHD (Shaw 2014 et seq.); PMDD-ADHD comorbidity (Dorani 2021, Broughton 2025); physiological state shaping thought content (Bower; Damasio; Critchley & Garfinkel; Barrett; Schwarz & Clore); the commercial gap; the anti-streak design rationale (converging user writing and community evidence); using HRV as multi-day context rather than as real-time trigger; the within-subject A/B mechanism for testing whether grounding reduces sycophancy.

**Weak on:** RSD as a mechanism (informal, not evidentiary); perimenopausal ADHD worsening (clinically claimed, empirically under-tested); the assumption that thoughts during dysregulation should generally be discounted (mood-congruent bias applies to ambiguous judgments, not accurate perception — Koor's prompt is deliberately designed not to discount, but the framing risk remains).

**Wrong on (and removed):** ADHD as chronic sympathetic activation. The autonomic literature leans hypo-arousal or dysregulated arousal, and polyvagal theory, often invoked to support this framing, is scientifically contested. Koor's current prompts and docs do not use this framing.

The single sharpest move Koor v1 makes: **reframe the physiological claim from "detect real-time dysregulation via HRV" to "use multi-day HRV trend, cycle phase, and sleep as context features that shape how the longitudinal-thought-history engine responds."** This is defensible, engineerable within the actual capabilities of wearable data (and exactly what mcPHASES provides), and doesn't overclaim. The "signal vs noise in thoughts" framing is narrowed from a general principle to a **specific, user-data-grounded intervention**: "you've had thoughts like this N times in this phase, here's what happened each time." That version of Koor is both more scientifically honest and, ironically, more differentiated — because the longitudinal thought graph is the thing nobody else owns.
